The basicsColour suggestionsMix a colour

Start with three things.

Hue

The colour family: red, yellow, blue and so on.

Value

How light or dark it is. Different values help shapes stand apart.

Saturation

How bright or muted it is. A quiet colour can make a bright one stand out.

Warm and cool colours

Reds, oranges and yellows usually feel warm. Blues and blue-greens usually feel cool. Compare them beside each other: one blue can feel warmer than another.

Tint, shade and tone

A tint adds white. A shade adds black. A tone usually adds grey. Watercolour gets lighter by adding water and letting white paper show through.

Choose colours that work together.

Let one colour take up most of the space. Use another in smaller areas, and save your strongest contrast for the part you want people to notice.

How do I mix this colour?

Pick a colour to get a starting recipe.

A “part” is the same-sized dab each time. These are rough starting mixes, not exact matches to the screen. Add the stronger colour slowly and test a small amount first. Use paints from the same medium.

What should I paint underneath?

For colour showing through gaps, try a small patch of a muted opposite underneath. A transparent layer over an opposite can become duller; a solid, opaque layer will hide it.

Why doesn’t my mix match?

Paint names can hide different pigments. A yellowish red and a pinkish red will not make the same purple. Try the suggested paint type, use a clean brush and check the mix after it dries.
